# Audit Report — style and discretion rules (binding)

> **THE HARD RULE, ABOVE ALL OTHERS: never name a real person in any report.**
> Roles only — leadership, owners, founders, the departed, subcontractors, the
> client's staff, and anyone the user named or has a personal relationship
> with. The only named subject allowed is the user in their own private
> personal/career report, because they asked. The only proper noun allowed is
> the client/project name, in report content only. Details in "Names" below.

## Voice

- Short sentences. Concrete nouns. One idea per sentence.
- As simple as possible, and no simpler — compress by omitting the
  unnecessary, never by blurring the true.
- No corporate euphemism ("learnings", "synergies", "opportunities for
  growth"). Say "the deadline was missed", not "timeline challenges emerged".
- Verdicts are allowed. An audit that concludes nothing is decoration.

## Evidence

- Every claim traces to a source: git evidence, a document, a meeting note, or
  an interview answer. Cite inline in prose ("commit history shows…", "the
  team reported…").
- Mark inference as inference: "this suggests", never stated as fact.
- Numbers beat adjectives: "4 tracking tools" not "too many tools".

## Discretion (people)

- Criticism attaches to roles, structures, and processes — not persons.
  Write "the technical lead role had no accountability structure; work could
  stall unseen for weeks", not a character assessment.
- Interview answers are paraphrased, not quoted, unless a quote is essential;
  never attribute a quote to a named individual. "One team member noted…" is
  the ceiling of attribution.
- When there was genuine dead weight, the report may say the role failed and
  what structure allowed it (silos, no check-ins, no visible output). State
  facts and the structural fix. No adjectives about the person, no names in
  the report body — titles/roles only.
- Team members put in awkward positions by others' failures are described as
  bearing costs, not as participants in the failure.

## Names — roles only, no exceptions

- **Every individual is referred to by role, never by name — in EVERY report,
  including the private personal-evaluation and career reports.** This holds
  for leadership, owners, founders, subcontractors, the client's staff, and
  anyone the user names in their answers or has a personal relationship with.
  Write "the founder of the firm you came from", not their name.
- **A personal relationship is not an exception — it is the exact case where a
  name is most likely to slip in.** If the user says "I value my relationship
  with `<Name>`", still write the role. Guard these hardest.
- Why: role-framing keeps the report reusable as advice for whoever holds the
  seat next, and keeps a private document safe if it is ever seen.
- When two people share a role, distinguish them by distinct role labels ("the
  agency owner" vs "the founder of the firm you came from"), never by name.
- The only name allowed anywhere is the CLIENT/project name, and only in report
  CONTENT (title, facts, prose) — never in file names, tooling, or this skill.
- **Before delivering any report, grep the output for every person-name that
  appeared in the sources and interview answers. Expect zero hits.** A single
  slipped name (even a friend's) is a defect; fix and re-export.

## Honesty calibration

- The user asked for blunt. Blunt means the report states what happened and
  what it cost. It does not mean venting: strip emotional language from
  answers, keep the factual core, preserve the severity.
- If an interview answer feels like "a cheap answer" (the user may say so
  themselves), find the real signal in it — e.g. "the team didn't give up"
  becomes "the project produced a battle-tested team, at the cost of burnout
  risk the company should not budget for again."
- Softening is for individuals. Systems, decisions, and companies get the
  unsoftened version.
