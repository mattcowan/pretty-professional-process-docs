# Outreach Method — audience, channel, competitive research and the outbound plan

Outreach is a **capability of the content strategy**, not a separate deliverable. The
default content-strategy outline plans *owned, organic* publishing (the site, blog, docs,
one or two social channels). Outreach adds the two lanes that publishing alone can't reach:
**earned/community** presence and **partnership/amplification**. This reference defines the
research method that grounds those lanes and the format of the outreach deliverables.

**The one thing that changed (why this exists).** Discovery is now heavily mediated by LLMs
and by community user-generated content, and a brand's *own* pages are the least-trusted
layer. Independent studies of AI product recommendations find a brand's own site is cited a
small minority of the time (roughly one in eight); the majority credits community discussion
(Reddit and forums foremost), independent reviews, and video. You cannot publish your way to
being recommended — you earn it by being genuinely discussed, reviewed, and used in the open,
on the third-party surfaces both models and humans trust. Outreach is the mechanism that
plants those signals. This reframes outreach from "get backlinks" to "get authentically
discussed and documented where models and humans both look."

## The Source Stack (the model to organize outreach around)

Think of everything an LLM (or a human researcher) assembles before naming a product as three
layers, each an outreach target:

1. **Owned / brand assets** — your site, docs, changelog, your own social. Necessary so the
   model can *understand* you (what the thing is, who it's for) but rarely cited as the
   authority. Job: be answer-shaped, factual, unambiguous about identity and audience.
2. **Validation layer — reviews & UGC** — community threads (Reddit, niche forums), directory
   reviews, showcases, independent walkthroughs/video. **This is where recommendations are
   actually won.** Models weight it because it reads as verified, un-astroturfed sentiment.
   Job: seed and sustain genuine community presence and independent coverage.
3. **Amplification — PR & media** — editorial coverage, roundup/"best X" listicles,
   newsletters. Job: get into the articles both humans and models mine.

The win condition is being **one of the ~3–7 sources a model uses to compose an answer** —
measured as citations/mentions, not search rankings.

## Step 1 — Research (do this before proposing any outreach)

Four inputs; every outreach recommendation must trace to one of them.

- **Audience research.** For each segment (by ROLE and by the *decision they came to make*),
  establish what they read, watch, and trust, and what language they use for the problem.
- **Channel research.** For each segment, find the actual watering holes — subreddits, forums,
  Discords, newsletters, YouTube channels, hashtags, showcase sites, conferences. **Verify each
  is active** (recent posts, real engagement) before recommending it; a dead channel in a plan
  is a defect. Record each channel's *norm/etiquette* — every venue punishes the wrong register.
- **Competitive-landscape research.** What alternatives the audience already uses or compares
  against, what gap the brand fills, and **where the competitors are being discussed** (their
  Reddit threads, their reviews) — those threads are outreach targets too.
- **Prompt research (new and essential).** Actually ask the LLMs (ChatGPT / Claude / Perplexity)
  the questions the audience would ask ("best way to do X on platform Y"). Record what gets
  recommended and **which sources are cited**. The cited sources are your concrete outreach
  target list; the gaps (questions with weak/no good answer) are content opportunities. Re-run
  periodically — it is the closest thing to a live scoreboard for GEO.

If web/LLM access is unavailable in the session, say so and mark the affected findings
`at-risk` (assumption, not evidence) rather than inventing channels.

## Step 2 — The three-lane channel & outreach plan

Replace the owned-only channel plan with three lanes. Every channel entry names its lane.

- **Owned** — the site, blog, docs, changelog, the brand's own social. Full control; least
  trusted; the job is to be findable and answer-shaped.
- **Community / earned** — participation (not spamming) in validation-layer venues: answering
  real questions, sharing genuinely useful posts, contributing to showcases, encouraging honest
  reviews and authentic user posts. Highest leverage, lowest cost — usually the right first lane
  for a solo maker or small brand.
- **Partnership / amplification** — integrations and co-marketing, guest content, getting into
  roundups and newsletters, seeding to educators/creators who teach the audience, PR to trade
  press, directory listings.

Each channel entry carries: **lane · target (specific venue) · why (which segment, grounded in
Step-1 evidence) · format that fits · cadence · the norm/etiquette · a first concrete action.**

## Step 3 — Messaging & positioning matrix

Not one tagline. One row per audience segment, with columns:

| Segment (role) | Problem *in their words* | The one value prop that lands | Proof that demonstrates it | Channel + format where they meet it |

The same product says a different first sentence to a wedding-stationery designer than to a
developer. The matrix keeps positioning honest (every value prop needs a proof) and directly
feeds both site copy and the outreach lanes (the "channel + format" column maps to Step 2).

## Step 4 — Channel norms (respect them or be ignored)

Every venue has an etiquette; violating it is worse than silence.
- **Community forums / Reddit / Discord:** participate as a peer, answer real questions, disclose
  affiliation, never drive-by promote. Bans are swift and the model-trust benefit evaporates if
  the signal reads as inauthentic.
- **Curated resource sites / showcases:** earn inclusion by being genuinely useful; you don't pitch
  your way in.
- **Trade press / newsletters:** pitch a *story* (something genuinely novel), not an ad; lead with
  the news hook, attach a demo.
- **Creators/educators:** offer value (early access, a genuinely useful angle for their audience),
  not a transaction.

## Evidence & ethics rules (hard constraints)

- **Ground everything in Step-1 research.** No channel, audience claim, or messaging line ships
  without a traceable source; mark inference as inference and unknowns `at-risk`.
- **Verify channels are live** before recommending them.
- **Never recommend astroturfing, undisclosed promotion, fake reviews, or sock-puppetry.** It is
  unethical and, per the UGC-trust finding, self-defeating: models and communities both punish
  inauthentic signals, and a caught brand loses the validation layer entirely.
- **Roles, never names** (inherited from the skill): outreach targets are venues and roles, not
  named individuals to "work."

## Directory / platform positioning (when the product lives in a directory)

Many products discover through a directory (an app store, a plugin/theme repository, a package
index). Treat the directory listing as owned-discovery infrastructure and research its actual
ranking mechanics rather than assuming — for each, capture: what the listing's search rewards
(title, keywords, tags, "tested/compatible" freshness), how ratings/reviews weigh vs. install
count, what recency/velocity signals help, and how the listing should link out to the site (and
the site back to the listing) so installs and reviews — which usually feed ranking — accrue in
one place. Soliciting genuine early reviews is almost always a top-priority action, because a
cold listing is often treated as low-rated until it earns its first strong reviews.

## What lands in the report (populated sections, not a separate research dump)

The *method* is here; the *findings* populate real content-strategy sections:
- Competitive/market findings → **Competitive & market landscape** section.
- Per-audience messaging → **Messaging & positioning matrix** section.
- Channel research + the three lanes → **Channel & outreach plan** section.
- Directory mechanics + review-solicitation + PR pitches → decisions and backlog/outreach actions.
- Prompt-research gaps → content-backlog items (answer pages for questions the LLMs currently
  answer badly).
